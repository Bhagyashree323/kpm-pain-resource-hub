Trigeminal Neuralgia Non-Surgical Care Guide

Direct Clinical Summary: Trigeminal neuralgia produces sudden, electric-shock facial pain on one side triggered by light touch, chewing, or speaking. First-line care uses carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine; image-guided trigeminal nerve blocks, radiofrequency ablation, or stimulation help when medicines fall short.

3-Step Patient Evaluation Checklist

  1. Log attack duration, facial division (forehead, cheek, jaw), and triggers.
  2. Obtain a brain MRI with thin cuts to evaluate vascular compression or secondary causes.
  3. Review medication response and sodium/liver labs before procedural options.
